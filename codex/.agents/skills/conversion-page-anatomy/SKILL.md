# Conversion Page Anatomy

This skill decides **what sections exist and in what order**. It does not decide the words inside them — that is [`high-converting-ui`](#pairing-with-high-converting-ui), and the pairing is mandatory, not optional.

Distilled from a survey of production landing-page, website, and ecommerce page structures — the section sequences and cadence rules that recur across high-converting long-form pages.

---

## The one idea everything hangs on

> **A long page is not a cognitive burden if every section is self-contained and terminates in the same single action.**

The instinct to shorten a page comes from confusing two different things: *choice load* (bad, and governed by Hick's Law) and *scroll length* (neutral). The canonical landing page runs 10,000px and repeats one identical CTA twelve times. It converts not despite the length but because a reader who becomes convinced at section 9 does not have to scroll back to act.

The corollary: **you do not know where conviction happens.** Different readers are persuaded by the pain section, the comparison table, the guarantee, or the FAQ. The page's job is to make every one of those moments actionable. That is why the CTA repeats and the proof brackets.

## How to use this skill

1. **Pick the page type** → [Page-type variants](#page-type-variants). This sets your section list and your target length. Getting this wrong is the most expensive error available: a contact page built to landing-page length, or a cold-traffic landing page built to contact-page length, both fail.
2. **Lay out the spine** → [The canonical spine](#the-canonical-spine). Mark each section required / optional / conditional.
3. **Apply the cadence rules** → [Cross-cutting cadence rules](#cross-cutting-cadence-rules). These are the rules that only exist at page scale and are the most commonly missed.
4. **Fill each slot** → [Block copy formulas](#block-copy-formulas) for the structure of the copy, then **`high-converting-ui`** for the actual wording. Every slot name in this skill is a label, never shippable copy.
5. **For a product gallery** → [PDP image carousel](#pdp-image-carousel), a separate artifact from the page itself.
6. **Auditing an existing page** → [Page audit checklist](#page-audit-checklist).

## The canonical spine

The full-length form. Every long-form template in the library is this list minus some sections; no template reorders it.

| # | Section | Status | Job |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Nav bar | required | Logo · phone · CTA. Page links **only** if this is a website page (see [nav is the tell](#nav-is-the-tell)) |
| 2 | Proof micro-line | required | Borrowed credibility *above* the headline, so the claim lands on a warm reader |
| 3 | Hero | required | 6 fixed slots, see [hero assembly](#the-hero-is-a-six-slot-assembly) |
| 4 | Proof strip | required | Second, heavier tier immediately below the fold |
| 5 | Pain / old way | conditional | Name the biggest pain, attack the status quo, tie in your differentiator |
| 6 | Proof section | required | First full-bleed proof block — stats, testimonials, case studies |
| 7 | Benefit block 1 | required | Eyebrow → benefit headline → media → mechanism → CTA |
| 8 | Benefit block 2 | required | Same shape, second value prop |
| 9 | Benefit block 3 | required | Same shape, third value prop |
| 10 | Proof section | required | Second full proof block |
| 11 | Differentiators | required | 3- or 6-cell grid: how you do it and why that's different |
| 12 | How it works | required | Exactly 3 steps. Job is *perceived effort reduction*, not information |
| 13 | Team / About | optional | Human trust. Drop on short pages |
| 14 | Guarantee | optional | Risk reversal. Marked "(Optional)" in the source, but see the note below |
| 15 | Proof strip | required | Repeated trust strip framing the FAQ |
| 16 | FAQ | required | 6 questions on B2B/website, 4 + media on ecom. Last objection sweep |
| 17 | Ancillary | website only | Blog posts / locations / resources. SEO surface and internal linking |
| 18 | Final close | required | Mirrors the hero — see [the close mirrors the hero](#the-close-mirrors-the-hero) |
| 19 | Footer | required | |

**On "optional" for the guarantee:** the source template marks it optional, but a guarantee is the single highest-leverage risk-reversal block available. Treat "optional" as "omit only if you genuinely cannot offer one," not as "nice to have."

**On the conditional pain section:** the landing template states it flatly. The homepage template hedges it — *"Address the largest pain point with unique benefit (only if relevant to your offering)"* — because a homepage serves mixed traffic that may not share one pain. A landing page for a specific offer can always name the pain, so on a landing page this section is effectively required.

## Cross-cutting cadence rules

These are the page-scale rules. They are what a section list alone will not give you, and they are where most pages fail.

### One CTA per section

`MAIN ACTION TO TAKE` appears **12 times across 10,095px** in the canonical template — roughly one per 800px of scroll. The rule is positional: *every section terminates in the CTA*. No section is a dead end.

Two constraints on this:

- **Always the same action.** There is no secondary-CTA ladder anywhere in the library. One action, repeated verbatim. Two competing CTAs *do* trigger Hick's Law; one repeated CTA does not.
- **This is reinforcement, not choice.** The choice set stays at n=1 regardless of instance count. Do not let a "reduce options" instinct delete CTA repeats.

### Social proof is tiered, at five depths

Three distinct tiers, each with fixed positions:

| Tier | Size | Content | Positions |
|---|---|---|---|
| Micro-line | ~20pt | Star rating, customer count, logo row | Above the H1, and again above the final close |
| Strip | ~32pt | Logo bar or trust row | Just below the hero fold, and again just above the FAQ |
| Full section | ~44pt | Testimonials, stats, case studies, UGC | 2–4×, between major sections |

The micro-line and strip each appear **twice**, bracketing the page: proof opens the hero, proof closes the FAQ, proof reopens the final close. Proof type is page-type specific — B2B gets *stats, testimonials, case studies*; ecom gets *UGC, testimonials, reviews* plus a scrollable UGC rail on the PDP.

### Friction copy goes below the button

The FUD-reducing line sits ~70px *beneath* every primary CTA, never above it. Measured consistently across templates (CTA 497px / friction 567px; 530/600; 474/544; 606/676).

Why the position matters: it answers the objection at the exact moment of hesitation, after the reader has looked at the button and paused. Above the button it is preamble; below it, it is a reassurance.

### The close mirrors the hero

The final section replays the hero's slots: proof micro-line → outcome headline → media → value-prop triple → CTA → friction line. Same six slots, slightly reordered. The last screen restates the first.

### Nav is the tell

The single structural difference between a landing page and a website page:

- **Landing page:** `Logo · phone · CTA` and nothing else. No page links. Every exit except the CTA is removed by design.
- **Website page:** adds `Page 1–4`. It has to let people browse.
- **Ecom:** `Anchor 1–4` (in-page scroll, not navigation) plus an announcement bar and cart counter.

The phone number is set larger than the logo (23pt vs 16pt) on B2B templates. For high-ticket B2B the call *is* the conversion, so the number outranks the brand.

### Length is set by funnel position

| Position | Length | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Cold traffic (landing, homepage) | ~10,000px | Full spine. Reader arrives unconvinced |
| Mid-funnel (service, SEO, PDP) | ~9,000px | Full spine, minus a section or two |
| Down-funnel (contact) | ~3,400px | Reader has already decided. Persuasion sections are noise |
| Browse (collection) | ~1,700px | Persuasion moves *into the card unit*, not the page |

Shortening a cold-traffic page in the name of "reducing cognitive load" is the most common way to break one.

## Page-type variants

### Landing — B2B / General (canonical, ~10,100px)
The full spine. Hero media may be image, video, **or** form.

### Landing — B2B form-in-hero (~8,300px)
- Hero media → `Lead Capture Form to capitalize on Above the Fold real estate` + 3 value props beside it
- Differentiator grid → **competitor comparison table** (Your Brand + 5 competitors × 5 points)
- Drops Team and Guarantee
- ⚠️ Form-in-hero conflicts with reciprocity. See [tensions](#known-tensions-and-the-ruling).

### Landing — Ecom General (~10,300px)
- Adds announcement bar: `HIGHLIGHT SOCIAL PROOF, REDUCTION OF FUDS, OR UNIQUE OFFER (i.e - Free Shipping, Money Back Guarantee, etc)`
- Anchor-link nav + cart counter
- Product/category grids: per-card tag + rating + inline `ADD TO CART - {PRICE}`
- Two 7-icon benefit belts (repeated at two depths)
- Money-back guarantee section
- `Additional Offer` block — secondary conversion path for non-buyers: *"selling products in bulk, entering people into a product quiz, sending people to a contact page"*
- All CTAs become `SHOP NOW`

### Landing / Website — Ecom PDP (~9,100px)
Buy box above the fold, then the full spine below it. Buy box order:
1. Star rating + `Rated X/X by XXX+ customers`
2. Product tag badge
3. H1 as `{Biggest Product Benefit} - {Product Name}` — benefit first, not product first
4. Price anchor pair: struck original → current → `You Save $X (X%)`
5. Short benefit description
6. Product image
7. 4 benefit chips
8. Numbered variant pickers: `1. Choose Color (X options)`, `2. Choose Size (X options)`, `View Size Guide`
9. Bundle ladder with `MOST POPULAR` on the middle tier
10. `Total:` — **anchor repeated**, struck price shown a second time
11. `ADD TO CART`
12. Trust trio directly beneath: `Guarantee | Return Policy | Shipping Policy`
13. Accordion: `Product Details / FAQ / How it Works / Shipping and Returns`

### Website — Homepage (~10,700px)
- Nav gains page links
- Headline formula upgrades to `Dream outcome (End Result, Time Period, Emotional Payoff)` — use this version, it is strictly better than the vaguer landing-page phrasing
- Pain section becomes conditional
- **Adds** `Services / Solutions / Courses you offer` (3-across, routes to sub-pages)
- **Adds** `Blog Posts / Ancillary Section / Locations Section` between FAQ and close
- Team widens to `Meet The Team / About Us Section`
- Drops Guarantee

### Website — Service / Location / Course (~9,400px)
The template to use for feature, use-case, and pillar pages. Two changes worth stealing:
- Eyebrow above H1 becomes `KEYWORD (FOR SEO PURPOSES) - H1 HEADER`
- Proof micro-line moves *below* the CTA rather than above the headline — search-entry traffic needs the keyword up top more than it needs borrowed authority

### Website — Contact (~3,400px)
Only 6 sections: form hero (5 value props) → proof section → how it works → proof strip → FAQ → footer. **No benefit blocks, no differentiators, no team, no guarantee, no urgency close. Three CTAs total** (606px, 1397px, 2241px). Someone on a contact page has already decided; persuasion sections actively get in the way.

### Website — Collection (~1,700px)
Abandons the persuasion spine. Rating bar → collection title + description → `Sort by` + availability/price filters → product card grid → footer. No CTA ladder.

Persuasion lives in the **card unit** instead: `BEST SELLER` tag + `4.8 (21 Reviews)` + price anchor trio (`$199` / struck `$242.00` / `Save $43`) + `SHOP NOW`. Every card carries a full miniature persuasion stack.

## Block copy formulas

**Hero headline** — `Dream outcome (End Result, Time Period, Emotional Payoff)`. Three parts. Not the product, not the company.

**Hero subhead** — negation hook + mechanism:
> `"Stop doing X" | "No more Y" | "Pain Point".` Our solution does X, Y, Z — articulate the main benefit **and explain how you do it in a proprietary or unique way.**

The "proprietary or unique way" clause is load-bearing. A benefit without a mechanism is a claim.

**Hero media — the 3 buying dimensions.** Media must satisfy all three:
- **Functional** — what it does, solves a specific problem or makes life easier
- **Emotional** — how it makes them feel: confident, secure, excited
- **Social** — how it makes them look: status, image, belonging

**Benefit block** (×3, identical shape) — `DREAM OUTCOME SUMMARY` eyebrow → `Value prop and Big Benefit #N ("WIIFM")` → media → mechanism sentence → CTA.

**Pain section** — *paint a vivid picture of the ideal prospect's pain points and expose the flaws of the status quo to acknowledge you know what they are going through, then complement with your unique value prop.* Note this is a status-quo attack, which is a different move from loss aversion (which targets the user's own assets at risk).

**How it works** — exactly 3 steps. Two headline patterns: count-forward (`Get your one-of-a-kind gift in 3 steps`) or effort-collapse (`Install in just 5 min with no special tools needed`). Each step = action headline + one detail line. **The last step is the emotional payoff, never a task** — `Watch their face light up`, not `Receive your order`.

**Comparison table** — your column gets claim + substantiating line; their column gets dismissive specifics. Rows are negated mirrors:
| You | Them |
|---|---|
| Mid-profile crown | No mid-profile crown |
| Slight curve brim | Flat brim |
| Easy to clean | Harder to clean |
| Hand stitched | Mass produced |

Headline can carry proof: `Why 80,000+ riders choose <brand>` — specific count + audience noun + verb + brand.

**Final close** — `Final recap - why should people take action today (create urgency)`. Fill the urgency with the reader's *existing pain persisting*, tied back to the pain section — not a manufactured deadline. Background image as overlay.

## PDP image carousel

A separate artifact from the page: a 1400×1400 square slide sequence for the product gallery. Declared order:

| # | Slide | Status |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Plain product shot | required |
| 2 | Problem visualization | if applicable |
| 3 | How it works | required |
| 4 | Social proof | required |
| 5 | Reduce FUDs / risk reversal | required |
| 6 | Comparison | if applicable |
| 7 | Lifestyle photos | required |

Per-slide internals:

- **Problem visualization** — `BEFORE | VS | AFTER`, 3 vs 3 mirrored rows. Example: headline `No more DIY cardboard & duct tape`, then *Inefficient & leaks air → Ultra energy efficient*, *Blocks natural light → Crystal clear visibility*, *Constantly falls apart → Made to last for years*. Parallel phrasing, same order both sides.
- **How it works** — 3 numbered steps, count in the headline, emotional payoff last.
- **Social proof** — UGC tiles, star rating, named attribution (first name + last initial), plus a headline count and exact review total, e.g. `Over 10,000 happy customers` / `Rated 4.9/5 - 1904 Total Reviews`.
- **Comparison** — two columns, negated mirror rows, claim + proof pairs.
- **Lifestyle** — product in context. Include an occasion grid if gift-relevant: `Perfect for ANY occasion` with use-case cells.

Additional slide layouts available in the framework: `What Makes Us Different?` (Our Product vs Other Products × 4 differences), `What's included in your kit?`, `Benefit-Driven Product Headline` + 6 benefits, `Highlight Your Risk Reversal or Guarantee`.

## Pairing with `high-converting-ui`

**This skill defines slots. `high-converting-ui` fills them.** Every label below is a slot name, not shippable copy:

| Slot in this skill | Apply from `high-converting-ui` |
|---|---|
| `MAIN ACTION TO TAKE` / `SHOP NOW` / `ADD TO CART` | E1 verb weight, E2 "my" not "your", A6 button states the outcome |
| `REDUCE FUDs` | C5 name the audience's *actual* fear; C1 volunteer the downside |
| `Rated X/X by XXX+ customers`, `4.8 (21 Reviews)` | B4 exact non-round numbers only |
| Price anchor, bundle ladder, `Total:` | B1 one number, B2 never in isolation, B3 strikethrough + % off |
| `1. Choose Color (X options)` | A4 swatches not dropdowns under 5; A2 pre-select `MOST POPULAR` |
| `Final recap ... (create urgency)` | D3 name the concrete loss, don't manufacture a deadline |
| Every `Image or video that...` | F1 show don't decorate, F2 bridge the imagination gap, F3 scale for transport |
| Bundle / tier selection | A2 smart defaults, A5 progressive disclosure, A3 cap simultaneous options |

Filled literally, this skill's placeholders ship exactly what `high-converting-ui`'s anti-pattern list bans — all-caps transactional verbs, round-number proof, generic trust badges. Run both.

### Known tensions and the ruling

**CTA repetition vs Hick's Law (A3).** Not a conflict. A3 governs *simultaneous distinct* choices; 12 instances of one identical action keeps the choice set at n=1. Apply A3 instead to the `Additional Offer` block (3 competing offers near the page bottom) and the collection page's simultaneous sort + availability + price controls — those are the real violations.

**Long scroll vs progressive disclosure (A5).** Different axes. A5 is interaction-depth disclosure; the spine is scroll-depth. The templates honour A5 where it counts — the PDP compresses `Product Details / FAQ / How it Works / Shipping and Returns` into accordions rather than dumping them in the decision zone. Rule: **collapse detail inside the decision zone; expand it below the fold.**

**Form-in-hero vs reciprocity (C2).** A real contradiction. C2 says gating value behind a form is like demanding a card before showing the menu. Form-in-hero is defensible only when the form *is* the value delivery (instant quote, calculator, free audit) or traffic is high-intent (paid search, contact page). For cold or content traffic, deliver value first. Treat as a conditional variant, never the default.

**7 benefit icons vs "cap at 5".** Icon rails are *scanned*, not chosen between. The ~5 cap applies to decision sets. 7 is fine in a belt.

**Thumbnail grids vs F3 "transport, don't inform".** Resolves cleanly — F3 permits thumbnails for dense comparison lists, which is exactly what a collection page is.

## Page audit checklist

Run against an existing page. For each "no," name the missing section or rule.

**Structure**
- [ ] Correct page type identified, and length in range for its funnel position?
- [ ] Does the nav match the type — page links on website pages, *no* page links on landing pages?
- [ ] All required spine sections present? Which are missing?
- [ ] Is section order preserved, with pain before the benefit stack?

**Cadence**
- [ ] Does *every* section end in a CTA? Count them — under one per ~800px is a flag
- [ ] Is it the same single action every time, with no competing secondary CTA?
- [ ] Social proof at all five depths, correctly tiered by size?
- [ ] Does proof bracket the page — above the H1, above the FAQ, above the close?
- [ ] Is the friction line *below* each primary CTA, not above?
- [ ] Does the closing section mirror the hero's six slots?

**Blocks**
- [ ] Hero headline carries end result + time period + emotional payoff?
- [ ] Subhead pairs a benefit with a *mechanism* ("how, uniquely")?
- [ ] Hero media satisfies functional + emotional + social?
- [ ] Exactly 3 benefit blocks, each with eyebrow → headline → media → mechanism → CTA?
- [ ] How-it-works is exactly 3 steps, last one an emotional payoff?
- [ ] Guarantee present, or genuinely impossible?
- [ ] Comparison rows are negated mirrors with claim + proof?

**PDP-specific**
- [ ] H1 leads with the benefit, not the product name?
- [ ] Price anchor shown twice — buy box and total?
- [ ] Trust trio directly under the add-to-cart?
- [ ] Carousel covers all 5 mandatory slides in order?

**Then run `high-converting-ui`'s conversion audit** on the contents of every slot.

## Anti-patterns

- A single CTA at the top and nothing else for 8,000px.
- Two competing CTAs in one section (this one *is* a Hick's violation).
- Social proof only in one place, usually a testimonial row halfway down.
- Friction/reassurance copy placed above the button.
- A cold-traffic landing page shortened to "reduce cognitive load."
- A contact page built to full landing-page length.
- Page links in a landing page nav — every one is an exit that isn't a conversion.
- A benefit stated with no mechanism ("we're faster" with no "because").
- How-it-works with 5+ steps, or a final step that's a task rather than a payoff.
- A comparison table where the rows aren't parallel, so the reader has to do the diffing.
- A collection page whose cards have no rating, tag, or price anchor — persuasion belongs in the card when it's not in the page.
- Filling this skill's slot labels in literally: `MAIN ACTION TO TAKE`, `Rated X/X`, `Product Benefit`.

> **The test:** open the page, pick any section at random, and ask *"if the reader were convinced right here, could they act without scrolling?"* If not, the spine is incomplete.
